Virtual UAV Engagement Arena

Flagship project · 2026

A virtual Fighting UAV competition, run on real ArduPilot autopilots.

A competition platform for fixed-wing UAV teams, modelled on the TEKNOFEST Fighting UAV format. Teams score by holding an opponent in their camera's hit zone, finishing a QR dive task, avoiding penalties and landing safely. Every aircraft is a real ArduPlane SITL autopilot, flown by the team's own code or by hand from a 3D browser cockpit, and an automatic referee scores every match from ground truth. Guests watch in 3D from one link.

  • Referee engine: lock-on validation (≥ 4 s window, ≥ 90 % in-zone, no gap > 0.3 s), geofence, telemetry watchdog, false-claim and collision penalties, warn / freeze / eject.
  • Four rulebooks: standard, TEKNOFEST mode, a defence scenario, and a standalone league.
  • League mode: fog of war, ground radar, hidden SAM sites, GPS and comms jamming, on-board ECM, and an unattended season runner that publishes standings and 3D replays.
  • Per-aircraft camera images streamed to each team, with recorded evidence clips for every lock-on review.
  • Fair play: per-team tokens, rate limits, server-side time, and an isolated MAVLink port for each team.
  • Multi-autopilot: ArduPilot and PX4 airframes, homologated before a match, spread across several computers.

The first merge is the most dangerous moment

With identical code, both aircraft pointed at each other within 20 s of take-off. Teams need a deconfliction rule before they need a lock-on algorithm.

Equal aircraft circle forever

Points come from asymmetry: slowing down to out-turn (ω = g·tan φ / V), using altitude, or extending and re-engaging.

Evasion is worth as much as attack

A predictable target gave up 11 locks in 10 minutes. Being locked costs −2, against +10 for the attacker.

Passive FM radar (PCL)

research

A passive coherent location system that detects aircraft using FM broadcast stations as illuminators. It uses RTL-SDR receivers, a DE10-Lite FPGA, cross-ambiguity processing with direct-path cancellation in Python, and ADS-B ground truth for validation. Next step: a coherent multi-channel array.

About

Background

I'm an electrical engineer turned systems builder. I am Assistant Professor and Head of the Information Systems Engineering Department at Near East University in Nicosia, Cyprus, where I split my time between teaching, research and department leadership. Before that I was Head of Software Engineering at RoboLabs, building embedded systems and ROS 2 control architectures for autonomous robots.

My work sits where software, signals and autonomy meet: autopilot-in-the-loop simulation, radar signal processing, wireless link analysis, and tools I can put in front of a real classroom the next morning.

I'm looking for engineering roles in robotics, UAV and simulation, and defence software.
